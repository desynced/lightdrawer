Example: insurance records

After a fire, a flood, or a break-in, an insurer may ask what you owned and what condition it was in. After storm damage, they may ask when the photos were taken. LightDrawer helps you keep a dated, checkable record of your photos and videos, both before anything happens and right after. It is free, there is no account, and your files are never uploaded.

Your photos are never uploaded. Only a fingerprint leaves your device. It is free, and nobody needs an account.

A fingerprint is a short code calculated from a file. Change even one pixel and the fingerprint changes completely. A LightDrawer receipt records that fingerprint with independent time witnesses, so anyone can check later that a file with that exact fingerprint existed by that time.

To keep things clear on this page, “receipt” means the LightDrawer receipt, and “purchase records” means the store receipts and invoices for things you bought.

If you are documenting a rental’s condition, or a home before a contractor starts work, see the example for repairs and move-outs.

The idea

  1. Before anything happens, you make a home inventory: photos and a video of your belongings. You stamp it.
  2. Once a year, or after a big purchase, you add to it and stamp the new files.
  3. If something happens, you photograph the damage as soon as it is safe, and stamp those photos the same day.
  4. When you file a claim, you can share the photos and their receipts. The insurer, or anyone else, can check them, even offline.

What this does and does not prove

It does show

  • that a photo or video with this exact fingerprint existed no later than the stamped times, and
  • that the file you share later is the same file, byte for byte, that you stamped.

It does not show

  • That you own something, or what it is worth. Keep purchase records, appraisals, and serial numbers.
  • What caused the damage, or that it is covered.
  • When a photo was taken. It shows the photo existed by the stamped time. An inventory stamped before a loss is the point: it shows you had those photos before the event.
  • That the photo was not edited before you stamped it. Stamp the originals.

LightDrawer is extra evidence of timing and integrity. It does not replace anything your insurer asks for, and it does not change your policy’s deadlines or claim steps. Ask your insurer or agent what documentation they want, and follow their process first.

How to do it, step by step

Make a home inventory

  1. Go room by room. Take a wide photo of each wall, then close-ups of valuable items. Open closets and drawers.
  2. Photograph details. Serial numbers, model labels, and the backs of electronics. Photograph purchase records and appraisals too, or scan them.
  3. Record a short video walk-through if you like, saying the date and what you are filming.
  4. Gather the files into one ZIP per inventory, for example home-inventory-2027-01.zip. Or stamp key photos one by one.
  5. Stamp it. Go to lightdrawer.org, drop in the ZIP, add a short note if you like (sample below), and download the receipt.
  6. Check it right away. Open Check a receipt and drop in the receipt and the ZIP you are keeping. It should match.

Store it somewhere a disaster will not reach

Keep the inventory and its receipt together, and keep a copy away from your home: a cloud folder, an email to yourself, or a USB drive with a relative. A record kept only on a computer in the house can be lost with the house.

Keep it current

Once a year, or after a large purchase, take new photos and stamp them as a new set. Keep the older sets too. Each one is a dated snapshot.

If something happens

  1. Stay safe first. Follow emergency instructions and your insurer’s guidance.
  2. Photograph the damage before cleanup, if it is safe and your insurer allows it. Include wide shots that show where you are, and close-ups.
  3. Stamp the photos the same day. Keep the originals unedited.
  4. Tell your insurer you have dated photos and receipts, and share them the way they ask.

If something does not match

What happenedWhat it usually meansWhat to do
The file matches its receiptIt is the exact file you stamped.Nothing.
A photo copied from your phone does not matchThe phone or computer converted it while copying (for example, from HEIC to JPEG).Find the copy you stamped. Next time, check right after stamping.
A photo sent by email, chat, or a claim portal does not matchMany apps and upload forms shrink or convert photos.Give the insurer your original and its receipt if they can accept it, and explain.
A ZIP does not matchIt was made again from the same files.Use the original ZIP.

Renaming a file does not change its fingerprint. Editing or re-saving it does.

Sample note for the receipt

The note is optional and saved inside your receipt, in your own words. Anyone you give the receipt to can read it.

Home inventory, January 2027, 18 Elm Road. Photos and video taken by me, Pat Morgan. Unedited.

Leave out serial numbers, account numbers, and anything else you would not want a reader to see. Those belong in the photos and your private records.

Sample message to an insurer or adjuster

I have photos of the damage taken on the day it happened, and a home inventory from last January. Each has a dated LightDrawer receipt. You can check any file against its receipt at lightdrawer.org/verify.html. I can share the original files however you prefer.

Good to know

  • Your photos stay private. Only the fingerprint leaves your device. Nobody can rebuild a photo from its fingerprint. Large videos are fine; they are read in pieces on your device.
  • Leave the public log box unchecked. A public entry is permanent and is not needed for an insurance record.
  • Timing. The timestamp authority’s record is in your receipt right away. The Bitcoin and certificate-log records finish over the next few hours. When you check the receipt online after that, the checker can fetch the finished records.
  • Built-in photo credentials. Some phones and cameras add Content Credentials. LightDrawer shows them if it finds them, but it does not check whether they are valid.
  • A second opinion. Any computer can calculate the same fingerprint without LightDrawer. On Windows, run certutil -hashfile home-inventory-2027-01.zip SHA256. On Mac or Linux, run shasum -a 256 home-inventory-2027-01.zip.
  • If LightDrawer disappears, your receipts still work. Each one has its own checker, and the time records live on independent public systems.
