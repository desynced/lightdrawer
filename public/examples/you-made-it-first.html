Example: show you made it first

You finished a song, a story, a logo, or a pitch. Months later, something very similar shows up somewhere else. The first question anyone asks is simple: when did you have it? LightDrawer helps you answer that with something better than “trust me.” It is free, you do not need an account, and your file is never uploaded.

Your work is never uploaded. Only a fingerprint leaves your device. It is free, and nobody needs an account.

A fingerprint is a short code calculated from a file. Change even one character or one pixel and the fingerprint changes completely. A LightDrawer receipt records that fingerprint with independent time witnesses, so anyone can check later that a file with that exact fingerprint existed by that time.

If you make art for a living and want a routine for drafts, sketches, and finished pieces, see the example for artists. This page is for anyone who has one idea, one demo, or one draft they want to date.

The idea

  1. You stamp your work the day you finish it, or the day you are about to share it. The receipt shows that exact file existed by then.
  2. You keep the file and the receipt together, unchanged, with a backup.
  3. If a question comes up later, you hand over the file and the receipt. Anyone can check that they match and see the stamped times, even offline.

Your file stays private the whole time. Only the fingerprint leaves your device, and nobody can rebuild your work from it.

What this does and does not prove

It does show

  • that a file with this exact fingerprint existed no later than the stamped times, and
  • that the file you show later is the same file, byte for byte, as the one you stamped.

It does not show

  • That you made it. A receipt says nothing about who created the file. It only shows when the file existed. Keep your other evidence too: project files, drafts, emails, and people who saw the work.
  • That nobody had it before you. It shows you had it by a time. It cannot show that someone else did not have something similar earlier.
  • That it is original. It says nothing about whether the work copies something else.
  • Ownership or rights. It does not register a copyright, a trademark, or a patent, and it is not a contract.

LightDrawer is extra evidence of timing and integrity. It does not replace copyright registration, a lawyer’s advice, or any filing your situation calls for. If something is at stake, talk to a lawyer about what evidence matters where you live.

How to do it, step by step

When you finish something

  1. Save a final copy. Export the song, save the story, or export the design the way you would share it. A PDF, a WAV or MP3, a PNG, or the original project file all work.
  2. Stamp it. Go to lightdrawer.org, drop the file in, and download the receipt. If you like, add a short note (sample below).
  3. Do not change that copy. If you keep working, work on a new copy. The stamped one is your record.
  4. Keep the receipt next to the file. Put them in the same folder with matching names, for example river-song-final.wav and river-song-final-receipt.zip.
  5. Back up both. Keep a second copy somewhere else: a cloud folder, a USB drive, or an email to yourself. A receipt is only useful if you still have the exact file that goes with it.

Before you share it or pitch it

Stamp the exact version you are about to send, even if you stamped an earlier draft. That way you can show what you sent and when you had it. Keep a short list of who you sent it to and on what day.

If you change it later

That is fine. Stamp the new version too, and keep both files with their receipts. A series of receipts can show how the work grew over time, which is often more convincing than a single date.

Several files at once

You can stamp files one by one. Or put them in a ZIP and stamp the ZIP. If you stamp a ZIP, keep that exact ZIP; a ZIP made again later from the same files will usually have a different fingerprint.

If something does not match

When you check a receipt, you drop in the receipt and the file. If they do not match, the file is not the same bytes as the one you stamped.

What happenedWhat it usually meansWhat to do
The file matchesIt is the exact file you stamped.Nothing.
No matchYou picked a different version, or the file was opened and saved again, converted, or compressed.Look for other copies in your backup. Try each one.
No match on a copy you downloaded from a chat, social, or cloud appMany apps shrink or convert files when you share or download them.Use your original export, not a shared copy.

Renaming a file does not change its fingerprint. Opening it and saving it again often does.

Sample note for the receipt

The note is optional. It is saved inside your receipt, in your own words. Anyone you give the receipt to can read it.

Final mix of “River Song,” written and recorded by me, Sam Rivera. Exported from my recording software on the day I finished it.

Keep it short and factual. Do not put passwords, private contact details, or anything you would not want a reader to see.

Sample line for a pitch or submission

I stamped this file with LightDrawer before sending it. The receipt is attached. Anyone can check it at lightdrawer.org/verify.html.

Good to know

  • Your work stays private. Only the fingerprint is sent to the witnesses. Notes stay inside your receipt and are only seen by people you give it to.
  • Stamp early, stamp often. A stamp made the day you finish is worth more than one made the day a dispute starts.
  • Timing. The timestamp authority’s record is in your receipt right away. The Bitcoin and certificate-log records finish over the next few hours. When you check the receipt online after that, the checker can fetch the finished records.
  • “No later than” is the key phrase. The witnesses show the file existed by a certain time. They do not show the moment you created it. Bitcoin’s clock can be off by up to about two hours.
  • The public log is optional and permanent. Leave the transparency-log box unchecked unless you want a public, permanent entry. It shows only a fingerprint, not your work, but it cannot be removed.
  • A second opinion. Any computer can calculate the same fingerprint without LightDrawer. On Windows, run certutil -hashfile song.wav SHA256. On Mac or Linux, run shasum -a 256 song.wav.
  • If LightDrawer disappears, your receipts still work. Each one has its own checker, and the time records live on independent public systems.
