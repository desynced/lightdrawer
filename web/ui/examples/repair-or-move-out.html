Example: photos before a repair or move-out

When you move in, move out, or let a contractor in, the same question comes up later: what did it look like before? Photos help, but people can argue about when they were taken. LightDrawer lets you date your photos and videos in a way anyone can check. It is free, you do not need an account, and your photos are never uploaded.

Your photos are never uploaded. Only a fingerprint leaves your device. It is free, and nobody needs an account.

A fingerprint is a short code calculated from a file. Change even one pixel and the fingerprint changes completely. A LightDrawer receipt records that fingerprint with independent time witnesses, so anyone can check later that a file with that exact fingerprint existed by that time.

This page is for renters, landlords, and homeowners dealing with a move or a repair. If you are documenting belongings or damage for an insurance claim, see the example for insurance records.

The idea

  1. You take photos or a video walk-through at move-in, before a repair, or at move-out.
  2. You stamp them the same day. The receipts show those exact files existed by then.
  3. You tell the other side that you have dated photos, and share them if needed.
  4. If there is a disagreement later, anyone can check the photos against the receipts, even offline.

What this does and does not prove

It does show

  • that a photo or video with this exact fingerprint existed no later than the stamped times, and
  • that the photo you show later is the same file, byte for byte, that you stamped.

It does not show

  • Where the photo was taken, or which unit it shows. Make that clear in the photos themselves (see the steps below).
  • When the photo was taken. It shows the photo existed by the stamped time. That is why stamping on the same day matters.
  • Who caused the damage, or what anything is worth.
  • That the photo was not edited before you stamped it. Stamp the originals from your phone, not edited copies.

LightDrawer is extra evidence of timing. It does not replace your lease, a move-in checklist, a signed inspection report, or any notice your lease or local rules require. For a deposit or repair dispute, check with a tenant-rights group or a lawyer about what evidence they need.

How to do it, step by step

Taking the photos

  1. Go room by room. Take a wide photo of each room, then close-ups of anything worn, broken, stained, or marked.
  2. Show where you are. Start with a photo of the front door with the unit number, or the street address. A short video walk-through, with you saying the date and address out loud, also helps.
  3. Include something for scale next to small damage, like a coin or a tape measure.
  4. Do not edit or filter the photos. Keep the originals as your phone saved them.

Stamping them the same day

  1. Gather the photos into one place. Copy them to a computer, or work from your phone’s browser.
  2. Choose: one by one, or one ZIP. For a few key photos, stamp each one. For a full walk-through, put all the photos in one ZIP and stamp the ZIP. If you stamp a ZIP, keep that exact ZIP.
  3. Stamp. Go to lightdrawer.org, drop in the file, add a short note if you like (sample below), and download the receipt.
  4. Check it right away. Open Check a receipt, then drop in the receipt and the copy you are keeping. It should match. This catches the most common problem: a phone or app quietly converting the photo, so the copy you keep is not the copy you stamped.
  5. Keep the file and receipt together, and back them up somewhere else, like a cloud folder or an email to yourself.

Telling the other side

Send your landlord, tenant, or contractor a short message (sample below). You do not have to send the photos right away. If you send the fingerprint on the day, they can check later that the photos you show are the same ones you told them about.

At move-out or after the repair

Repeat the same steps, in the same order, from the same spots. Matching “before” and “after” photos, each with a receipt, tell the clearest story.

If something does not match

What happenedWhat it usually meansWhat to do
The photo matches its receiptIt is the exact file you stamped.Nothing.
No match on a photo copied from your phoneThe phone or computer converted the photo while copying it (for example, from HEIC to JPEG).Find the copy you actually stamped. Next time, check right after stamping.
No match on a photo sent through a chat or social appMost chat and social apps shrink photos.Use the original, not the shared copy.
No match on a ZIPThe ZIP was made again from the same photos.Use the original ZIP. A new ZIP usually has a different fingerprint.

Renaming a file does not change its fingerprint. Editing, cropping, or re-saving it does.

Sample note for the receipt

The note is optional and saved inside your receipt, in your own words. Anyone you give the receipt to can read it.

Move-in walk-through, Apartment 4B, 120 Oak Street. Photos taken by me, Jordan Lee, on my phone the morning I got the keys. Unedited.

Sample message to a landlord or contractor

Hi, I took photos of the apartment’s condition today, March 3, and made a dated receipt for them with LightDrawer. The fingerprint is below. I can share the photos and receipt any time. You can check them at lightdrawer.org/verify.html.

Fingerprint: (paste the code from your receipt)

Good to know

  • Your photos stay private. Only the fingerprint leaves your device. Nobody can rebuild a photo from its fingerprint.
  • Your note is not public, but it travels with the receipt. Leave out anything you would not want the other side to read.
  • Leave the public log box unchecked unless you want a permanent public entry. It shows only a fingerprint, but it cannot be removed.
  • Timing. The timestamp authority’s record is in your receipt right away. The Bitcoin and certificate-log records finish over the next few hours. When you check the receipt online after that, the checker can fetch the finished records.
  • Built-in photo credentials. Some phones and cameras add Content Credentials to photos. LightDrawer shows them if it finds them, but it does not check whether they are valid.
  • A second opinion. Any computer can calculate the same fingerprint without LightDrawer. On Windows, run certutil -hashfile photo.jpg SHA256. On Mac or Linux, run shasum -a 256 photo.jpg.
  • If LightDrawer disappears, your receipts still work. Each one has its own checker, and the time records live on independent public systems.
