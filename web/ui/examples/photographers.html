Example: for photographers

Your photo turns up on a website you never licensed it to. Or a client says the images you delivered were different. Or someone claims a shot was staged after the fact. In each case you want to show that this exact image existed, unchanged, on a given day. LightDrawer gives you a dated receipt for each file that anyone can check. It is free, you do not need an account, and your images are never uploaded.

Your images are never uploaded. Only a fingerprint leaves your device. It is free, and nobody needs an account.

A fingerprint is a short code calculated from a file. Change even one pixel or one bit of metadata and the fingerprint changes completely. A LightDrawer receipt records that fingerprint with independent time witnesses, so anyone can check later that a file with that exact fingerprint existed by that time.

If you collect images from other people as a reporter, see the example for journalists. If you also make illustrations or other art, the example for artists covers drafts and finished pieces.

The idea

  1. You stamp your originals the day you shoot, when you copy them off the card.
  2. You keep those originals unchanged and edit copies, or use an editor that leaves originals alone.
  3. You stamp what you deliver or publish, so you have a record of each version that left your hands.
  4. If a question comes up, you show the original and its receipt. Anyone can check them, even offline.

What this does and does not prove

It does show

  • that an image with this exact fingerprint existed no later than the stamped times, and
  • that the file you show later is the same file, byte for byte, as the one you stamped.

It does not show

  • That you took the photo. A receipt says nothing about who made it. Your full set of originals from a shoot, with frames before and after, is often the strongest evidence of that.
  • When the shutter was pressed. It shows when the file existed. Stamping the same day keeps that gap small.
  • That the scene was not staged or that the image was not edited before you stamped it.
  • Ownership or a license. It does not register a copyright and it is not a licensing agreement.

LightDrawer is extra evidence of timing and integrity. It does not replace copyright registration, your contracts, or legal advice. For a licensing or copyright dispute, talk to a lawyer about what evidence matters where you work.

How to do it, step by step

After each shoot

  1. Copy the card to your computer as you normally do. Keep the files exactly as the camera wrote them.
  2. Put the shoot in one ZIP, for example 2027-05-14-harbor-shoot.zip. This gives you one receipt for the whole day. Or, for a few key frames, stamp each file on its own.
  3. Stamp it. Go to lightdrawer.org, drop in the ZIP or the image, add a short note if you like (sample below), and download the receipt.
  4. Check it right away. Open Check a receipt and drop in the receipt and the copy you are keeping. It should match.
  5. Keep the ZIP and its receipt together with your archive, and back both up somewhere else. If you stamped a ZIP, keep that exact ZIP. Zipping the same files again later usually gives a different fingerprint.

While you edit

Many photo editors keep your edits in a catalog or a separate file and leave the original untouched. Some settings write changes back into the original, which changes its fingerprint. After your first edit session, check a few originals against their receipts to be sure your setup leaves them alone.

When you deliver or publish

Stamp the final exports you send to a client or post online. Keep them with their receipts. That way you can show both what you shot and what you delivered.

If you re-edit later

That is fine. Stamp the new export too and keep both. Each version has its own receipt.

If something does not match

What happenedWhat it usually meansWhat to do
The original matches its receiptIt is the exact file you stamped.Nothing.
An original no longer matchesYour editor wrote changes into it, or this is a different copy.Look for the untouched copy in your backup, and check your editor’s settings.
A copy from a website does not matchWebsites and social apps resize and re-compress images, which makes a new file.This is normal. Show your original and its receipt instead.
A ZIP does not matchIt was made again from the same files.Use the original ZIP.

Renaming a file does not change its fingerprint. Editing pixels or metadata does.

Sample note for the receipt

The note is optional and saved inside your receipt, in your own words. Anyone you give the receipt to can read it.

Harbor shoot, May 14, all frames as copied from the card. Photographer: Alex Kim. Not edited.

Sample line for a contract, invoice, or delivery email

The delivered images were stamped with LightDrawer on the delivery date. Receipts are available on request and can be checked at lightdrawer.org/verify.html.

Sample line for a licensing request

I can share the original file from this shoot and its dated LightDrawer receipt, made on the day of the shoot.

Good to know

  • Your images stay private. Only the fingerprint leaves your device. Nobody can rebuild an image from its fingerprint. Large files are fine; they are read in pieces on your device.
  • Content Credentials. Some cameras and apps add Content Credentials to images. LightDrawer shows them if it finds them and notes them in the receipt, but it does not check whether they are valid.
  • Leave the public log box unchecked unless you want a permanent public entry. It shows only a fingerprint, but it cannot be removed.
  • Timing. The timestamp authority’s record is in your receipt right away. The Bitcoin and certificate-log records finish over the next few hours. When you check the receipt online after that, the checker can fetch the finished records.
  • A second opinion. Any computer can calculate the same fingerprint without LightDrawer. On Windows, run certutil -hashfile shoot.zip SHA256. On Mac or Linux, run shasum -a 256 shoot.zip.
  • If LightDrawer disappears, your receipts still work. Each one has its own checker, and the time records live on independent public systems.
